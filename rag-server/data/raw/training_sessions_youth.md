# Training Sessions — Youth-Adapted (U14 / U16)

## 1. Youth Session Design Overview

Section summary: Youth basketball sessions (ages 13–16) must account for shorter attention spans (10–12 minutes per drill block maximum), developing motor patterns, lower physical load capacity, and the primary goal of enjoyment alongside skill development — a youth player who stops enjoying the sport is lost to the game.

Youth sessions differ from adult sessions in five key ways:
1. **Shorter drill blocks:** No single drill block exceeds 10–12 minutes. Change activities frequently to maintain focus.
2. **Lower cognitive complexity:** Every drill has at most one decision node. Never ask youth players to simultaneously read two defensive actions.
3. **Lower physical load:** Sprint volume is reduced by 40–50% relative to adult pre-season sessions. Youth players are still developing bone density and connective tissue — overloading increases injury risk significantly.
4. **Gamified elements:** Attach a competition or scoring element to every drill block. Youth players engage more deeply with activities that have a winner.
5. **Positive correction culture:** Errors are corrected with a "what to do" instruction, not a "what not to do" criticism. Youth players who feel criticised disengage.

**Recommended session frequency:** 2–3 sessions per week. No more than three competitive sessions per week at U14/U16 level.
**Recommended session duration:** 60 minutes (U14) to 75 minutes (U16).

## 2. Youth Fundamental Session (U14) — 60 Minutes

Section summary: This U14 session introduces the three most fundamental basketball skills — passing, dribbling, and layup finishing — through short, gamified drill blocks designed to maintain attention and generate high repetitions within the session's time limit.

**Session objective:** Build correct passing mechanics, improve weak-hand dribbling comfort, and develop reliable two-step layup footwork on both sides of the basket.

---

### Phase 1 — Activation and Warm-Up (10 minutes)

- **3 minutes: Fun warm-up game — Dribble Knockout:**
  - Every player has a ball and dribbles within the three-second lane. Players try to knock other players' balls out of bounds while protecting their own. Last player dribbling inside the lane wins.
  - Coaching purpose: develops protective dribbling and court awareness without it feeling like a drill.
- **4 minutes: Dynamic stretching:**
  - Leg swings (8 each leg), hip circles (8 each direction), jumping jacks (20), arm circles (10 each direction).
  - Keep it moving — no more than 30 seconds on any single stretch.
- **3 minutes: Passing warm-up in pairs:**
  - Players pair up. One ball per pair. Chest pass back and forth from three metres. Coach watches for "wrist snap to the target" and corrects palm-pushers.

### Phase 2 — Skill Block 1: Passing (12 minutes)

- **Drill: Three-Man Weave (Drill PA-01) — Youth version (10 minutes):**
  - Walk through the weave pattern twice at 50% speed with no ball. Players point to where they are going before they go.
  - Full weave at game pace. Players earn one team point for every basket scored off the weave without stopping.
  - Target: 20 team points in 10 minutes. If the team reaches 20 before time is up, the record is set and new players start.
- **Quick debrief (2 minutes):** Coach asks players: "What do we do right after we pass?" Answer: "Cut and run!" Reinforce the pass-and-cut habit verbally.

### Phase 3 — Skill Block 2: Dribbling (12 minutes)

- **Drill: Cone Weave — Youth version (Drill BH-02 simplified, 8 minutes):**
  - Youth version: only two moves used at each cone — crossover at cone 1 and 3; hesitation (no ball transfer) at cone 2 and 4.
  - Race format: two lines race to complete the weave and reach the basket first. First player to score a layup after the weave wins the round.
  - Five rounds. Teams of two. This format keeps energy high and ensures all players get reps.
- **Weak-hand dribble challenge (4 minutes):**
  - Every player dribbles a full length of the court using only their weak hand (left hand for right-handed players).
  - Players who lose control start over from the baseline. Players who complete the full length earn one point.
  - Three attempts each. Coach notes which players need additional weak-hand development.

### Phase 4 — Skill Block 3: Layup Finishing (12 minutes)

- **Drill: Mikan Drill — Youth competition version (Drill FI-01, 8 minutes):**
  - All players do the Mikan Drill simultaneously at the basket. Count makes aloud: 1, 2, 3...
  - First player to reach 10 consecutive makes wins. Players who miss reset their count to zero.
  - Three rounds. Coach observes footwork: "Left foot for right-hand layup. Right foot for left-hand layup." Correct any player using the wrong gather foot immediately.
- **Give-and-Go into Layup (Youth Play Y-01, 4 minutes):**
  - Practice the Give-and-Go (Youth Play Y-01) with a passers at the top and a cutter going to the basket.
  - Emphasis: cut starts the instant the ball leaves your hands. No pause.
  - Five give-and-go layups per player. Rotate passers and cutters.

### Phase 5 — Competitive Application (8 minutes)

- **3-on-3 half-court game — "Magic Number" format:**
  - Three vs. three. First team to seven points wins. One point per basket scored.
  - Rule: any basket scored off a pass-and-cut (Give-and-Go) is worth two points. This incentivises the skill taught in the session.
  - Coach tracks Give-and-Go attempts and acknowledges players who attempt them (regardless of outcome).

### Phase 6 — Cool-Down (6 minutes)

- **Static stretching (3 minutes):** Quad stretch (20 seconds each leg), hamstring stretch (20 seconds), shoulder stretch (20 seconds). Keep it brief — youth attention span does not extend to long cool-downs.
- **Team huddle (3 minutes):**
  - Coach states one thing the team did better today than last session (specific: "Your passing was sharper — fewer bad throws").
  - Coach states one focus for next session (specific: "Next time we work on the left-hand layup").
  - Finish with the team's chant or fist bump ritual.

## 3. Youth Intermediate Session (U16) — 75 Minutes

Section summary: This U16 session introduces screen-and-cut actions, catch-and-shoot mechanics from the three-point arc, and basic team defensive positioning — building on the fundamentals of the U14 level while adding the tactical layer appropriate for 14-to-16 year-old players.

**Session objective:** Develop reliable screen-and-cut execution (Simple Pin-Down, Youth Play Y-02), introduce the concept of spacing for catch-and-shoot opportunities, and practise the basic Shell Drill defensive positioning.

---

### Phase 1 — Activation and Warm-Up (10 minutes)

- 3 minutes: Dribble Knockout (same as U14 — effective at all youth levels as a warm-up).
- 4 minutes: Dynamic stretching (same sequence as U14 but slightly longer holds — 30 seconds each).
- 3 minutes: Two-man passing in motion — pairs pass while jogging from baseline to baseline and back. Chest pass only.

### Phase 2 — Skill Block 1: Screen and Cut Fundamentals (15 minutes)

- **Screen-setting technique (5 minutes):**
  - Stationary practice: players pair up. One player sets a screen on the other's shoulder. Screener must be completely stationary before the cutter uses the screen.
  - Coach walks through: "Feet shoulder-width apart, arms crossed on your chest, do not lean into the cutter."
  - 10 screens per player in the screener role. Coach corrects every moving screen.
- **Simple Pin-Down execution (Drill / Youth Play Y-02, 10 minutes):**
  - Use two groups at two baskets simultaneously.
  - Each group: C (5) sets pin-down for SG (2) who curls to the wing. PG (1) passes to SG for a catch-and-shoot jump shot.
  - Five curl shots, five flare shots per player. Three rotations so each player fills each role.
  - Competition: which group reaches 10 made jump shots first?

### Phase 3 — Skill Block 2: Catch-and-Shoot Mechanics (12 minutes)

- **Five-Spot Catch-and-Shoot — U16 version (Drill SH-01, 10 minutes):**
  - Use only three spots initially (north wing, top of the key, south wing) — not the corners, which are pure three-point shots and harder to reach with proper mechanics at this age.
  - One passer, one shooter. Shooter moves through three spots, three shots each (nine attempts per rotation). Four rotations total.
  - At U16, target 40% from three spots (4 of 9 makes acceptable; 6 of 9 is excellent).
- **Coaching focus (2 minutes):** Quick check of all five mechanical checkpoints (see basketball_training_sessions.pdf, Section 2.1): stance, grip, alignment, upward force, follow-through.

### Phase 4 — Competitive Application (15 minutes)

- **Youth BLOB practice (Youth Play Y-03, 5 minutes):**
  - Teach and run the Youth BLOB (Y-03) from both sides of the baseline.
  - Four reps with no defence (execute the screens and cut cleanly). Two reps with passive defence.
- **3-on-3 half-court game (10 minutes):**
  - Same "Magic Number" format as U14 but now with an additional two-point bonus for any basket scored off a screen-and-cut action (using a pin-down screen from the session).
  - Coach calls out: "That was a screen-and-cut!" to reinforce the connection between practice and game application.

### Phase 5 — Basic Defence Intro — Shell Drill (10 minutes)

- **Shell Drill — Phase 1 only (Drill DEF-04, 10 minutes):**
  - Youth Shell Drill: four offensive players (no defense yet) move the ball by passing. Coach walks through defensive positioning verbally as the ball moves. "Ball goes to the wing — where does the help-side defender go? Down, toward the paint."
  - After the verbal walkthrough: add four defenders. Ball movement only (no dribbling, no cutting). Defenders must maintain correct positioning on every pass.
  - Do not advance to Phase 2 (cutting) in the same session. One defensive concept per session at U16.

### Phase 6 — Cool-Down (8 minutes)

- Static stretching: full lower and upper body (30 seconds each stretch).
- Team huddle: one positive observation, one focus for next session. End with the team chant.

## 4. Youth Session Load and Scheduling Guidelines

Section summary: Youth session volume must be managed carefully to prevent overuse injuries and burnout — these scheduling guidelines protect long-term player development.

- **Maximum sprint volume (U14):** 6 full-court sprints per session. Anaerobic sprint work should not be the focus of U14 sessions.
- **Maximum sprint volume (U16):** 10 full-court sprints per session. Conditioning drills should be gamified (relay races, weave competitions) rather than pure sprint intervals.
- **Session frequency:** No more than 3 competitive sessions per week at any youth level. Recovery days are non-negotiable.
- **Game-to-practice ratio:** For U14, every practice session should result in two or more game situations (scrimmages) so that the skills learned feel immediately relevant.
- **Attention span management:** Change activity every 10–12 minutes maximum. If players' engagement drops, move to the next activity even if the drill is not complete.
- **Positive error correction:** When correcting a youth player's error, use the "what to do" format: "Bring the ball above your head on the outlet pass" — not "Don't drop the ball below your ears." Youth players encode positive instructions more effectively than negative ones.
