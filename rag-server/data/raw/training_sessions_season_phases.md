# Training Sessions — Season-Phase Templates

## 1. Season-Phase Session Overview

Section summary: Training sessions must be calibrated to the phase of the season — pre-season sessions are fitness-heavy with high volume, in-season sessions balance skill sharpness with recovery management, playoff sessions are low-volume and precision-focused; using the wrong session type for the wrong phase is the most common cause of player fatigue and injury.

Each section below provides a full session template for the corresponding season phase, including session objectives, time allocation, drills, and load targets. All sessions use the five-phase structure (activation → skill → competitive application → conditioning → cool-down).

---

## 2. Pre-Season Session (8–12 Weeks Before First Game)

Section summary: Pre-season sessions emphasise fitness development, individual fundamental skill-building, and system installation; conditioning volume is at its highest point of the year, with progressive overload managed carefully to prevent soft-tissue injuries.

**Session objective:** Build the aerobic and anaerobic base that will sustain performance for the full season, while installing the team's offensive and defensive systems through repetition.

**Session duration:** 90 minutes
**Load target:** RPE 7–8 (Rate of Perceived Exertion on a 1–10 scale). Players should finish sessions feeling worked but not broken.
**Frequency:** 5–6 sessions per week. Mandatory one full rest day per week.
**Progressive overload rule:** Do not increase total sprint volume by more than 10% above the previous week.

---

### Pre-Season Sample Session: Week 3

#### Phase 1 — Activation and Warm-Up (15 minutes)

- 5 minutes: light jogging + side shuffles + back-pedals around the court perimeter.
- 5 minutes: dynamic stretching sequence — leg swings (10 each), hip circles (10 each), ankle rotations (10 each), thoracic rotations (5 each side), arm circles (10 each).
- 5 minutes: basketball-specific activation — zig-zag defensive slides (full court, four runs), skip passes in motion (two-man skip-pass relay), two-ball dribble warm-up at 50% intensity.

#### Phase 2 — Individual Skill Work (25 minutes)

- **Ball-handling (10 minutes):**
  - Two-Ball Stationary Dribble (Drill BH-01): 3 sets × 3 phases (90 seconds each).
  - Cone Weave Change of Direction (Drill BH-02): 5 runs per player. Focus: attack after the move, not just the move itself.
- **Shooting mechanics (10 minutes):**
  - Five-Spot Catch-and-Shoot (Drill SH-01): 2 full rotations per player. Track makes; document results to monitor weekly improvement.
- **Finishing (5 minutes):**
  - Mikan Drill (Drill FI-01): all players simultaneously for 2 sets of 20 consecutive makes or 60 seconds, whichever comes first.

#### Phase 3 — System Installation (20 minutes)

- **Week 3 focus: Introduce HORNS FLAT (primary half-court set).**
  - Step 1 (5 min): Whiteboard walk-through — coach draws every movement on the board. Define primary, counter, safety valve verbally before players touch the court.
  - Step 2 (7 min): Walk-through at half speed without defence. No dribbling — pass-and-cut only. Coach pauses to correct spacing and screen angles.
  - Step 3 (8 min): Walk-through with passive defence. Defenders shadow offensive players without contesting. Focus on reading the defensive position.
- Coaching note: Do not add a second play in the same session. Install one play completely before introducing another.

#### Phase 4 — Physical Conditioning (20 minutes)

- **Sprint intervals (12 minutes):** 4 sets of 4 court sprints (baseline to baseline). Rest 20 seconds between sprints, 90 seconds between sets. Target RPE 8–9 during sprint sets.
- **Lateral quickness (8 minutes):** Defensive Slide Ladder (Drill DEF-01): 30 seconds on / 15 seconds off, four rounds. Add ball-shadow on rounds 3 and 4.

#### Phase 5 — Cool-Down (10 minutes)

- Static stretching: quad stretch (30 sec each leg), hamstring stretch (30 sec), hip flexor lunge (30 sec each side), calf stretch (30 sec each), shoulder cross-body (30 sec each).
- Breathing regulation: 2 minutes slow diaphragmatic breathing.
- Team debrief: one positive, one improvement point. Keep to 2 minutes.

## 3. In-Season Session (Game Weeks)

Section summary: In-season sessions maintain skill sharpness and system execution while managing fatigue; the primary practice day (two to three days before a game) is full-intensity but shorter than pre-season; the day-before-game session is a walkthrough only; the day-after-game is optional recovery only.

**Session objective:** Maintain the skills and systems installed in pre-season. Address opponent-specific preparation. Prevent fatigue accumulation across a long season.
**Session duration:** 60 minutes (primary practice day) / 45 minutes (walkthrough day).
**Load target:** RPE 6–7 on primary practice days. RPE 4–5 on walkthrough days.

---

### In-Season Sample Session: Primary Practice Day (Game in 2 Days)

#### Phase 1 — Activation and Warm-Up (8 minutes)

- 3 minutes: dynamic stretching only (no jogging — preserve the legs).
- 5 minutes: basketball-specific activation — two-ball dribble warm-up, skip passes in motion, 10 form-shooting close-range shots per player.

#### Phase 2 — Skill Maintenance (15 minutes)

- **Shooting maintenance (8 minutes):** Five-Spot Rapid Fire (Drill SH-02): 3 rounds per player. Track makes — in-season target is maintaining pre-season percentages, not exceeding them.
- **Ball-handling maintenance (7 minutes):** Pressure Dribble (Drill BH-03): 3 pairs, two rounds each (30 seconds per round). Moderate intensity — not maximum effort.

#### Phase 3 — Opponent-Specific Preparation (20 minutes)

- **10 minutes — Scouting preparation:** Run the opponent's featured set play with your defence. Coach calls the opponent's play; defensive unit executes their prepared coverage.
  - Repeat three to four times until execution is clean. This is the highest-priority segment of the in-season practice.
- **10 minutes — Set-play review:** Run the team's called plays for the upcoming game at walk-through then competitive speed.
  - Primary HTO set: HORNS FLAT (2 reps walkthrough, 3 reps competitive).
  - Primary ATO: Pre-selected ATO play for end-of-quarter situations (3 reps).

#### Phase 4 — Competitive Application (12 minutes)

- **3-on-3 half-court:** Full competition. Three possessions per team, then rotate. Coach tracks execution errors (missed reads, incorrect spacing). Verbally correct after each possession.

#### Phase 5 — Cool-Down (5 minutes)

- Static stretching (lower body only — quads, hamstrings, hips).
- Team debrief: game plan one-sentence summary. "Tomorrow we focus on: [one defensive key, one offensive key]."

---

### In-Season Day-Before-Game Session (Walkthrough — 45 Minutes)

**Load target:** RPE 3–4. No sprinting, no competitive drills.

- Phase 1 (5 min): Light ball-handling and form shooting only.
- Phase 2 (20 min): Walk-through of every called play for tomorrow's game — each play executed once at 50% speed. Verbal confirmation of each player's assignment.
- Phase 3 (10 min): Walk-through of opponent's tendencies — show two of their key sets and walk through the defensive coverage.
- Phase 4 (10 min): Free shooting, individual work, team meeting. Coach delivers the game plan summary (two offensive priorities, two defensive priorities). No conditioning.

## 4. Playoff Session (Post-Season Competitive — Low Volume, High Precision)

Section summary: Playoff sessions eliminate all non-essential volume and focus exclusively on sharp execution of the team's highest-confidence plays and their specific defensive assignments for the upcoming opponent; player freshness is the primary variable, not skill development.

**Session objective:** Arrive at each playoff game with full physical readiness and maximum mental focus. No new plays. No new schemes. Execute what the team already knows at their highest level.
**Session duration:** 50 minutes maximum.
**Load target:** RPE 4–5. No heavy conditioning. Players should finish practice feeling fresher than when they arrived.
**Key principle:** In the playoffs, confidence matters more than novelty. Run the plays you know best, not the plays that would theoretically be hardest to defend.

---

### Playoff Sample Session (Day Two Before Game)

#### Phase 1 — Activation (8 minutes)

- 3 minutes: dynamic stretching — emphasise hip and ankle mobility.
- 5 minutes: shooting activation — 10 form-shooting shots per player from close range, then 5 catch-and-shoot threes per player from their preferred spots.

#### Phase 2 — Playbook Sharpening (25 minutes)

- **Primary half-court set (10 minutes):** Run the team's highest-confidence half-court set (e.g., HORNS FLAT) at full speed. Focus: perfect spacing, timing, and the correct option selection. If an error occurs, stop and correct immediately — do not let a mistake run to completion in a playoff practice.
  - 6 reps with live defence. Coach designates coverage for each rep so the team executes every counter at least twice.
- **ATO and special situations (10 minutes):** Run every ATO play the team may need in the upcoming game:
  - End-of-clock ATO (under 5 seconds): 4 reps of the primary ATO.
  - Last-two-minutes ATO (needing a three): 4 reps of the three-point ATO.
  - Inbound situation from the opponents' end (BLOB): 4 reps.
- **Opponent defensive key (5 minutes):** Walk through the one defensive adjustment the team needs to make for this opponent. One clear, memorable instruction per player. No complex new schemes.

#### Phase 3 — Quick Competitive Segment (10 minutes)

- 2-on-2 pick-and-roll competition (5 minutes): Maintain competitive sharpness with low-body-stress drill. No full-court sprints.
- Team shooting: all five players shoot simultaneously from their designated spots. Track makes in 3 minutes. Targets: a make percentage that matches or exceeds the team's pre-season target — ends practice on a positive note.

#### Phase 4 — Cool-Down (7 minutes)

- Full static stretching sequence (lower and upper body).
- Breathing regulation: 2 minutes diaphragmatic breathing in the locker room.
- Mental preparation: Coach delivers a one-sentence affirmation based on the team's strongest performance from the season — a factual reminder of what they are capable of.

## 5. Session Load Monitoring Guidelines

Section summary: Session load must be tracked weekly to prevent over-training and injury — a sudden spike in weekly training volume is the leading predictor of soft-tissue injury in basketball players.

- **Weekly load tracking:** Record the total duration and RPE of every session. Weekly load = sum of (session duration × RPE) for all sessions that week.
- **10% rule:** Do not increase total weekly load by more than 10% above the previous week. If a game week adds unexpected load (extra practices, travel), reduce practice intensity accordingly.
- **Recovery signals:** Players who report RPE 9–10 for two consecutive sessions need a mandatory rest day, regardless of schedule. Chronic fatigue is not solved by pushing through it.
- **Playoff load target:** In the two weeks before a playoff game, weekly load should be 70–75% of peak pre-season load. Players arrive at games with a "freshness surplus."
