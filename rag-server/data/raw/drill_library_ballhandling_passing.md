# Drill Library — Ball-Handling and Passing

## 1. Ball-Handling and Passing Drill Library Overview

Section summary: This section contains ten drills covering ball-handling and passing skills, each formatted with name, objective, setup, execution, coaching cues, and progressions from easier to harder so coaches can select and scale drills to their team's ability level.

---

## 2. Ball-Handling Drills

### Drill BH-01: TWO-BALL STATIONARY DRIBBLE

- **Name:** Two-Ball Stationary Dribble
- **Objective:** Develop simultaneous weak-hand and strong-hand dribbling coordination, improve finger-pad control, and build weak-hand strength for game-speed ball-handling.
- **Setup:** Each player needs two basketballs. Players stand in a triple-threat stance on any flat surface — half-court, outside, gym floor. No cones or equipment needed beyond two balls per player.
- **Execution:**
  - Phase A — Both balls together: Dribble both balls simultaneously, bouncing them at the same time. Keep both dribbles below the knee. Eyes up — do not look at the balls. Duration: 30 seconds.
  - Phase B — Alternating rhythm: Dribble both balls in alternating rhythm — as one ball goes down the other comes up. Duration: 30 seconds.
  - Phase C — One high, one low: Dribble the strong-hand ball at waist height and the weak-hand ball at knee height simultaneously. Duration: 30 seconds.
  - Rest 15 seconds between phases. Complete three full sets (A+B+C = one set).
- **Coaching cues:**
  - "Finger pads, not palms" — players who use the palm lose control on the weak-hand ball.
  - "Eyes on the basket" — point at a spot on the wall and require all players to look at it throughout.
  - "Push, don't slap" — the dribble is a push-down motion, not a slap.
- **Progressions:**
  - Easier: Use one ball only; focus on weak-hand stationary dribble at knee height, 60 seconds.
  - Harder: Add lateral movement — two-ball dribble while sliding sideways five steps, then back. Maintain control during movement.
  - Hardest: Two-ball full-court dribble — walk the full court length while maintaining both dribbles simultaneously.

### Drill BH-02: CONE WEAVE CHANGE OF DIRECTION

- **Name:** Cone Weave Change of Direction
- **Objective:** Train dribble change-of-direction moves (crossover, between-the-legs, behind-the-back) at game speed while maintaining court vision and attacking the next cone with the first dribble after the move.
- **Setup:** Set five cones in a straight line, one to two metres apart, from one end of the key to the half-court line. One ball per player. Players line up at the baseline.
- **Execution:**
  - Player dribbles toward the first cone using their strong hand.
  - At each cone, execute a designated change-of-direction move: Cone 1 = crossover; Cone 2 = between-the-legs; Cone 3 = behind-the-back; Cone 4 = spin dribble; Cone 5 = hesitation (stutter step, no ball transfer).
  - After the last cone, attack at full speed toward the basket for a finish.
  - Next player starts when the previous player passes cone 3.
  - Complete five full runs per player per drill block.
- **Coaching cues:**
  - "Attack the cone, not around it" — the player should approach within half a metre of the cone before executing the move.
  - "Low on the move, high on the attack" — dribble height drops during the move and rises when attacking after the move.
  - "One dribble, one step" — immediately attack in the new direction after the move; no settling.
- **Progressions:**
  - Easier: Use only the crossover at every cone. Focus on keeping the dribble low during the transfer.
  - Harder: Add a passive defender at the final cone who contests the finish.
  - Hardest: Player calls the move type themselves (no pre-set pattern) based on the coach's signal at each cone.

### Drill BH-03: PRESSURE DRIBBLE (ON-BALL DEFENDER)

- **Name:** Pressure Dribble
- **Objective:** Train ball-handlers to maintain dribble control and decision-making under live on-ball defensive pressure — the most game-accurate ball-handling drill.
- **Setup:** Two players, one ball. Ball-handler starts at the top of the key (approx. `{x: 370, y: 255}`). Defender is one metre away in a defensive stance. Half-court space. No cones.
- **Execution:**
  - Ball-handler dribbles freely within the half-court space for 30 seconds. The defender applies live pressure — they may reach for the ball, mirror movement, and contest drives but they may not foul or steal through the body.
  - Ball-handler's goal: maintain control, change of pace and direction, and attack the paint at least twice within the 30 seconds.
  - After 30 seconds, swap roles.
  - Complete three rounds per pair.
- **Coaching cues:**
  - "Change of pace creates separation — not just change of direction."
  - "Use your body — shield the ball with your non-dribbling arm and body on drives."
  - "Find the gap, not the help" — attack where the defender is absent, not where help is waiting.
- **Progressions:**
  - Easier: Defender uses only shadow pressure (mirrors movement, no reaching). Ball-handler focuses on change-of-pace dribble only.
  - Harder: Ball-handler must attack the basket and finish within the 30-second window at least three times. Defender applies maximum legal pressure.
  - Hardest: Add a second passive defender at the basket. Ball-handler must make a read on the drive — finish or kick to an imaginary or real third player.

### Drill BH-04: CHAIR DRIBBLE SERIES

- **Name:** Chair Dribble Series
- **Objective:** Train game-speed change-of-direction moves at a stationary screen (represented by a chair), replicating the ball-handler's action in pick-and-roll and DHO situations.
- **Setup:** One folding chair placed at the elbow (approx. `{x: 190, y: 188}`). One ball per player. Players line up at the top of the key.
- **Execution:**
  - Player dribbles from the top of the key toward the chair at game speed.
  - At the chair, execute the designated move: (Round 1) use the chair on the right — attack left. (Round 2) use the chair on the left — attack right. (Round 3) reject the chair — change direction before reaching it and attack the opposite lane.
  - After each move, attack at full speed to the basket for a finish.
  - Four repetitions per player per round. Three rounds.
- **Coaching cues:**
  - "Set up your defender before you use the screen" — dribble directly at the 'chair' first, then make your move.
  - "Shoulder through the gap" — the ball-handler's shoulder should almost touch the chair on the use; this forces the defender off the screen.
  - "Speed kills" — the move only creates an advantage if the ball-handler accelerates immediately after.
- **Progressions:**
  - Easier: Slow the approach to half speed. Focus only on footwork — drop step at the chair, push off the inside foot.
  - Harder: Add a live defender at the chair who simulates hedging (steps out aggressively). Ball-handler must attack through or around the hedge.
  - Hardest: Add a second defender at the basket who contests the finish. Ball-handler must read: finish strong or kick to a passive wing player.

## 3. Passing Drills

### Drill PA-01: THREE-MAN WEAVE

- **Name:** Three-Man Weave
- **Objective:** Train chest passes and bounce passes in motion, develop court vision, and build the habit of passing ahead of the receiver's body (leading the target) during transition.
- **Setup:** Three players, one ball, full court. Players align in three lines at the baseline: left lane, middle, right lane.
- **Execution:**
  - Middle player starts with the ball. Middle passes to either side (left or right), then cuts behind that player to fill their lane.
  - The receiver passes to the opposite player and cuts behind them. Players continue weaving across the court, passing the ball ahead without stopping.
  - No player ever dribbles. All passes are catch-and-pass continuations.
  - When the team reaches the far basket: the player who receives the final pass finishes with a layup. The other two fill the lanes for a three-man weave back.
  - Complete four full court-and-back sequences as a group.
- **Coaching cues:**
  - "Pass and sprint" — the pass triggers the sprint, not the other way around.
  - "Lead the receiver" — pass to where the receiver will be, not where they are.
  - "Eyes up on the catch" — players who look at the ball lose track of the runner ahead.
- **Progressions:**
  - Easier: Walk through the weave at half speed without a basket finish. Focus on the cut pattern only.
  - Harder: Add the rule that no player may slow down while passing — all passes happen at full sprint speed.
  - Hardest: Five-man full-court weave with two balls simultaneously.

### Drill PA-02: SKIP PASS DECISION DRILL

- **Name:** Skip Pass Decision Drill
- **Objective:** Train the overhead skip pass used to reverse the ball against a zone defence or to exploit a rotating help defender, with emphasis on pass accuracy and the decision about when to skip.
- **Setup:** One ball, five players in a half-court zone-offence alignment (3-2: three along the arc, two in corners). Coach stands at the three-point line with a cone.
- **Execution:**
  - Ball starts at the top of the key with PG (1).
  - Coach moves the cone to represent the zone defender.
  - PG makes two-to-three passes along the arc, reading the cone's position.
  - When the cone (zone defender) shifts toward the ball side: PG executes a skip pass to the opposite corner or wing using an overhead throw.
  - Receiver catches and shoots immediately.
  - Five repetitions with the skip going left; five going right. All players rotate positions.
- **Coaching cues:**
  - "Two hands above the ears" — the skip pass starts with both hands above the head; dropping the ball below ear level before the pass creates interception risk.
  - "High arc on the skip" — a flat skip pass can be intercepted by a zone defender who reads the rotation; a higher arc clears the zone middle.
  - "Ball goes where the defender isn't — read the cone."
- **Progressions:**
  - Easier: Remove the zone movement; coach calls "skip left" or "skip right" verbally. Focus on pass mechanics only.
  - Harder: Replace the cone with an active zone defender. PG must make the skip-or-hold decision under live defensive pressure.
  - Hardest: Add a second live defender. The skip pass must be made over both defenders without interception.

### Drill PA-03: NO-LOOK PASS DRILL

- **Name:** No-Look Pass Drill
- **Objective:** Develop the ability to deliver accurate passes while looking in a different direction — training the brain to decouple eye direction from passing direction, which creates defensive confusion in game situations.
- **Setup:** Three players, one ball, half-court. Passer stands at the top of the key. Two receivers stand at the north wing and south wing approximately three metres from the passer.
- **Execution:**
  - Passer makes eye contact with the north wing receiver but passes to the south wing receiver. Alternate sides every two repetitions.
  - The passer must hold eye contact with the fake target for at least half a second before throwing.
  - The real receiver signals with a hand target — the passer passes to the hand target without looking.
  - Ten repetitions per player in the passer role.
- **Coaching cues:**
  - "Look early, throw late" — establish the fake eye contact first, then release the pass.
  - "The fake must be believable — commit to looking at the wrong target."
  - "Receiver: give a hand target; the passer is not looking, they need a tactile reference."
- **Progressions:**
  - Easier: Passer is stationary; receivers are stationary. Focus purely on the eye decoupling mechanics.
  - Harder: Passer dribbles before the no-look pass. Add one dribble pull-up option — passer chooses between dribbling to the basket or making a no-look pass based on the defender's movement.
  - Hardest: Add a live defender on the passer who reacts to the eye direction. The no-look pass must beat the defender's reaction.

### Drill PA-04: OUTLET PASS RAPID FIRE

- **Name:** Outlet Pass Rapid Fire
- **Objective:** Train the rebounder's pivot-and-outlet mechanics under time pressure — the outlet pass after a defensive rebound must be executed within two seconds to initiate transition; this drill builds that habit at speed.
- **Setup:** Two players, one ball, near the basket. One player stands at the south block (approx. `{x: 145, y: 323}`) as the rebounder. Second player stands at the north wing (approx. `{x: 330, y: 113}`) as the outlet target. Coach or third player stands at the top of the key to simulate a defender.
- **Execution:**
  - Coach tosses the ball off the backboard. Rebounder jumps and secures the rebound with two hands.
  - Immediately upon landing, rebounder pivots away from the simulated defender (pivot toward the sideline, away from the middle) and delivers an overhead outlet pass to the wing player.
  - Wing player catches and holds the ball, simulating PG receiving the outlet.
  - Reset and repeat. Ten repetitions, then switch positions.
  - Target: outlet pass delivered within two seconds of the rebound landing.
- **Coaching cues:**
  - "Chin the ball" — secure the rebound with both elbows out and the ball under the chin to protect it before pivoting.
  - "Pivot away from pressure, not into it."
  - "Overhead, not sidearm" — sidearm outlet passes are more easily stolen; the overhead pass clears defenders.
- **Progressions:**
  - Easier: No simulated defender. Focus on the pivot mechanics and pass accuracy only.
  - Harder: Add a live defender who steps toward the rebounder on landing. Rebounder must make the outlet while protecting the ball from a live reach.
  - Hardest: Rebounder makes the outlet, then immediately sprints to fill the middle lane — three-man transition drill triggers from the outlet.
