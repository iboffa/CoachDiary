# Drill Library — Defence and Transition

## 1. Defence and Transition Drill Library Overview

Section summary: This section contains twelve drills covering 1v1 on-ball defence, team defensive rotations, and transition (both offence-to-defence and defence-to-offence), each formatted with name, objective, setup, execution, coaching cues, and progressions from easier to harder.

---

## 2. One-on-One Defence Drills

### Drill DEF-01: DEFENSIVE SLIDE LADDER

- **Name:** Defensive Slide Ladder
- **Objective:** Build the lateral quickness, correct sliding footwork (push-slide, never cross the feet), and endurance needed to maintain on-ball defensive positioning against a live dribbler — the most fundamental defensive conditioning drill.
- **Setup:** Use the lane lines as boundaries. Five cones spaced two metres apart in a straight line between the north lane line and the south lane line (across the paint). No ball needed initially.
- **Execution:**
  - Player starts in a defensive stance at the north lane line. On the signal, they slide laterally to the south lane line and back, touching each cone en route with their nearest hand.
  - Slide: push off the back foot, slide the lead foot, reset hips. Never cross the feet.
  - 30 seconds of continuous sliding, 15 seconds rest. Four rounds.
  - After two rounds, add a ball-shadow element: a partner walks laterally with a ball, and the defender mirrors them without crossing their feet.
- **Coaching cues:**
  - "Wide base — feet wider than shoulder-width at all times. A narrow stance cannot change direction."
  - "Stay low — hips must stay at the same height throughout the slide; do not bob up and down."
  - "Push, don't step — the trail foot pushes off the floor; the lead foot does not step before the trail pushes."
- **Progressions:**
  - Easier: Slow the slide to 50% speed. Focus only on foot pattern and hip height. No cones — use the paint as the width boundary.
  - Harder: Partner ball-shadow at game speed. The defender must react to the ball-handler's changes of direction.
  - Hardest: Live 1-on-1 in a restricted zone (the three-second lane only). Ball-handler tries to drive past the defender. Defender uses sliding footwork exclusively.

### Drill DEF-02: CLOSEOUT AND CONTAIN

- **Name:** Closeout and Contain
- **Objective:** Train the two-phase closeout sequence — the sprint and the controlled slow-down — and the ability to contain a live drive after the closeout so that the perimeter defender does not give up a layup off an aggressive closeout sprint.
- **Setup:** Defender starts at the north block (approx. `{x: 145, y: 188}`). Ball-handler starts at the north wing (approx. `{x: 330, y: 113}`) and receives a pass from a coach at the top of the key. One ball.
- **Execution:**
  - Coach passes to the ball-handler on the north wing.
  - Defender sprints from the block toward the ball-handler on the wing. At two metres away from the ball-handler, the defender chops their feet (quick short steps) and arrives with high hands and a low centre of gravity — not at full sprint.
  - Ball-handler has three options: catch-and-shoot, jab step and shoot, or one dribble and drive.
  - Defender reads and responds: contest the shot or drop into a contain stance on the drive.
  - Five reps per player pair. Switch roles.
- **Coaching cues:**
  - "Sprint, chop, contest — in that exact order. Chopping too early wastes time; chopping too late creates a charge."
  - "High hands on arrival — both hands above shoulder height to contest the shot without fouling."
  - "Drop step on the drive — when the ball-handler puts it on the floor, drop the nearest foot toward the baseline to cut off the drive angle."
- **Progressions:**
  - Easier: Ball-handler is stationary — no drive option. Defender practises only the closeout mechanics.
  - Harder: Ball-handler can drive in either direction with two dribbles. Defender must contain.
  - Hardest: Ball-handler is a live shooter-driver with no restriction. Defender closes out and contests a real shot or contains a real drive, both leading to a finish attempt.

### Drill DEF-03: ONE-ON-ONE WING ISOLATION

- **Name:** One-on-One Wing Isolation
- **Objective:** Develop the complete package of 1v1 on-ball defence skills — stance, footwork, forcing direction, and contesting the finish — against a live ball-handler in the most common half-court 1v1 situation.
- **Setup:** Two players, one ball. Ball-handler starts at the north wing (approx. `{x: 330, y: 113}`). Defender starts an arm's length away in a defensive stance. Full half-court access.
- **Execution:**
  - Ball-handler may use any dribble move and attack in either direction. Three dribbles maximum before a shot must be taken. If no shot is possible in three dribbles, possession ends.
  - Defender forces the ball-handler toward the baseline (forcing baseline rule — most defensive systems force baseline). No reaching for the ball from behind.
  - Possession ends on a made basket, a miss (rebound ends the drill), a steal, or three dribbles without a shot.
  - Five possessions per player in each role.
- **Coaching cues:**
  - "Force direction — you decide where the ball-handler goes, not them."
  - "On the drive: your shoulder is in front of theirs — beat the dribbler to the spot, not by speed but by angle."
  - "No straight lines — make the ball-handler change direction on every step. Each change costs them a tenth of a second."
- **Progressions:**
  - Easier: Ball-handler can only drive in one pre-declared direction. Defender focuses on maintaining position against a predictable attack.
  - Harder: No dribble limit for the ball-handler. Full game-speed 1v1 until a basket or defensive stop.
  - Hardest: Add a passive screener at the elbow. Ball-handler may use one screen. Defender must navigate the screen.

### Drill DEF-04: SHELL DRILL (TEAM DEFENCE, 4-ON-4)

- **Name:** Shell Drill (4-on-4)
- **Objective:** Develop team defensive positioning, help-side positioning, rotations on ball movement, and communication — the Shell Drill is the most widely used team defence teaching tool because it isolates positioning and rotation without live scoring pressure.
- **Setup:** Four offensive players in a 2-2 alignment: two at the wings (approx. `{x: 330, y: 113}` and `{x: 330, y: 398}`), two in the corners (approx. `{x: 87, y: 73}` and `{x: 87, y: 438}`). Four defenders match up. Ball starts at one wing.
- **Execution:**
  - Phase 1 — Ball movement only: Offence passes the ball around (no dribbling, no cutting) using chest passes and overhead skip passes. Defenders must maintain correct positioning on every pass — ball-you-man triangle for off-ball defenders; active closeout for the on-ball defender.
  - Phase 2 — Add cutting: Offence can now cut. Defenders must communicate cuts ("cut!") and provide help-side positioning on cutters entering the paint.
  - Phase 3 — Full competition: Offence may dribble, cut, and shoot. No screen actions. Defence competes to stop every scoring attempt.
  - Five minutes per phase.
- **Coaching cues:**
  - "Ball-you-man — if you can't see both the ball and your man with a straight line through your body, your position is wrong."
  - "One pass away = denial; two passes away = help-side. These rules are non-negotiable."
  - "Call every movement: 'ball', 'skip', 'cut', 'help' — silence is a defensive error."
- **Progressions:**
  - Easier: Phase 1 only — no cutting, no dribbling. Focus entirely on help-side positioning and on-ball closeout.
  - Harder: Add one screen action in Phase 3 per possession. Defenders must communicate and execute screen coverage.
  - Hardest: Full 5-on-5 Shell Drill including all half-court offensive actions.

### Drill DEF-05: PICK-AND-ROLL COVERAGE REPS

- **Name:** Pick-and-Roll Coverage Reps
- **Objective:** Train a specific ball-screen defensive coverage (drop, hedge, switch, or ICE) repeatedly against a live P&R until the on-ball and screener defenders execute the assigned coverage automatically without verbal reminders.
- **Setup:** Three offensive players (PG with ball at the top of the key `{x: 370, y: 255}`, C as screener at the elbow `{x: 190, y: 323}`, and one corner shooter `{x: 87, y: 438}`). Three defenders. Coach designates the coverage before each rep.
- **Execution:**
  - Coach calls coverage: "Drop" / "Hedge" / "Switch" / "ICE."
  - PG (1) sets up the screen and uses it. Defenders execute the designated coverage.
  - On-ball defender and screener defender communicate ("screen coming left!") before the screen is set.
  - After each rep, coach gives one correction only — identify the single most important error.
  - Eight consecutive reps of the same coverage until both defenders execute without error, then switch to a different coverage.
- **Coaching cues:**
  - Drop: "Level of the screen, not below it — the big drops to screen level, not to the paint."
  - Hedge: "Jump and recover — the big comes out aggressively then sprints back; do not camp on the hedge."
  - Switch: "Communicate the switch before the screen — 'switch!' called late is a mistake."
  - ICE: "On-ball defender forces away from the screen before it is set — the ICE starts before the action."
- **Progressions:**
  - Easier: Offensive players execute at half speed. Defenders focus on footwork and positioning without time pressure.
  - Harder: PG reads the defensive coverage and selects the counter — PNP option if the defence drops; drive-and-lob if the hedge is late.
  - Hardest: Full live 3-on-3 with no coverage call. Defenders must communicate and decide coverage in real time.

## 3. Team Defence Drills

### Drill DEF-06: HELP AND RECOVER (DRIVE AND KICK DEFENCE)

- **Name:** Help and Recover
- **Objective:** Train the off-ball defensive reaction to a dribble drive — the help defender must stunt toward the driver, then recover to their assigned player before the kick-out pass arrives.
- **Setup:** Three offensive players: PG (1) drives from the top of the key, SG (2) is in the north wing (approx. `{x: 330, y: 113}`). Three defenders: X1 on PG, X2 on SG, X4 as the interior help defender starting near the paint.
- **Execution:**
  - PG (1) drives hard toward the paint.
  - X4 steps out to stunt toward PG's drive (one to two steps, not full commitment).
  - PG kicks to SG (2) on the north wing as X4 is stunting.
  - X2, on SG, must close out on the kick-out while X4 recovers to their assignment.
  - The sequence must be executed within one second of the kick-out: X2 closes out, X4 recovers.
  - Eight repetitions. Switch positions.
- **Coaching cues:**
  - "Stunt, don't commit — X4 shows presence to hesitate the driver, then recovers immediately."
  - "X2: be on the way before the ball arrives — anticipate the kick-out, don't react to it."
  - "Recovery is a sprint — not a shuffle. Run to recover, slide to defend."
- **Progressions:**
  - Easier: No live ball. PG walks toward the paint; X4 practises the stunt timing and recovery footwork.
  - Harder: PG drives and can choose to kick to either wing. X4 must stunt and both X2 and X3 must be ready to close out.
  - Hardest: Add a second kick-out option (corner). Drive-kick-drive-kick sequence. All four perimeter defenders must execute two consecutive stunt-and-recover cycles.

## 4. Transition Drills

### Drill TR-01: THREE-ON-TWO INTO TWO-ON-ONE FULL COURT

- **Name:** Three-on-Two Into Two-on-One Full Court
- **Objective:** Train the three-on-two primary break read (attack the first defender, pass to the open wing, or pull up), and immediately train the two-on-one defensive conversion as the two previous defenders now push the other way — both offensive and defensive transition skills in one drill.
- **Setup:** Full court. Three offensive players start at one baseline (O1 in the middle, O2 on the left wing, O3 on the right wing). Two defenders start in the opposite half-court. One ball.
- **Execution:**
  - O1, O2, O3 attack. X1 and X2 defend the 3-on-2.
  - After the possession ends (make, miss, or steal), X1 and X2 immediately become the offensive players and two of O1/O2/O3 (the designated two) convert to defenders — the other one stays.
  - X1 and X2 push the ball the other way for a 2-on-1 against the single defender.
  - Continue alternating. Team in transition earns a point for every scored basket in transition.
- **Coaching cues:**
  - "3-on-2: ball in the middle, wings beyond the arc. The wing fills all the way to the corner, not just to the elbow."
  - "Ball-handler: attack the first defender's chest. Pass when they commit — not before."
  - "2-on-1 offence: the ball-handler attacks the basket. If the defender stops the drive, pull up for a pull-up jumper."
- **Progressions:**
  - Easier: Run 3-on-0 first (no defenders) to teach lane-filling and passing positions. Add 3-on-2 once lanes are understood.
  - Harder: Add a trailer (fourth offensive player) who can trail the 3-on-2. The drill becomes 4-on-2 with a potential "secondary break" pass to the trailer.
  - Hardest: Limit the 3-on-2 team to three dribbles total. They must make all their decisions and passes within three dribbles.

### Drill TR-02: SECONDARY BREAK TRIGGER

- **Name:** Secondary Break Trigger
- **Objective:** Train the transition from primary break to secondary break to half-court offence in one continuous sequence — players learn to read the defence's recovery speed and choose between attacking early and slowing into the half-court set.
- **Setup:** Five offensive players start at one baseline. Five defenders start at the half-court line (giving the offence a head start). One ball. Coach starts the drill with a whistle.
- **Execution:**
  - On the whistle, offence pushes the ball up the court and attacks.
  - Defenders sprint back to set up in the half-court.
  - If the offence has a clear numbers advantage at half-court: attack immediately (primary or secondary break).
  - If the defence has recovered five defenders: offence calls the half-court set and runs a designated play (coach pre-selects: "HORNS" or "ZIP" or another set).
  - The read decision (attack vs. play call) must be made by PG (1) within two seconds of crossing half-court.
  - Five repetitions. Switch offence and defence.
- **Coaching cues:**
  - "PG: look up at half-court. Count defenders. Two back = attack. Five back = call the play."
  - "In early offence: do not slow down to call a play — attack the disorganised defence before it sets."
  - "The play call is a signal, not a pause — call it and immediately start the first action."
- **Progressions:**
  - Easier: No defenders. Offence practises the transition from running to half-court set without the decision pressure.
  - Harder: Defenders start at the three-quarter court line rather than half-court — the offence has less time to assess.
  - Hardest: Coach signals which set to run (HORNS, Floppy, etc.) mid-transition. PG must hear the call, communicate it to teammates, and execute while still in motion.

### Drill TR-03: OUTLET AND FILL DRILL

- **Name:** Outlet and Fill Drill
- **Objective:** Train the complete transition sequence from a defensive rebound — secured rebound, outlet pass to PG, PG pushes, wings fill lanes — so that the first four seconds of transition are automatic and do not require coaching in game conditions.
- **Setup:** Five offensive players in a half-court defensive alignment. Coach throws the ball off the backboard to simulate a missed shot. Five players are designated: one rebounder (C / 5), outlet target (PG / 1), two lane-fillers (SG / 2, SF / 3), and one trailer (PF / 4).
- **Execution:**
  - Coach throws the ball off the backboard. C (5) rebounds with both hands.
  - C (5) pivots away from the imaginary defender and delivers an overhead outlet to PG (1) at the outlet zone (approx. `{x: 330, y: 113}` or `{x: 330, y: 398}`).
  - PG (1) pushes the ball up the middle lane at full speed.
  - SG (2) and SF (3) fill the outside lanes, running to the corners or wings on the far end.
  - PF (4) trails in the middle at three to four metres behind PG.
  - Drill ends when PG reaches the far three-point arc.
  - Repeat six times, then rotate positions.
- **Coaching cues:**
  - "C: chin the ball, pivot in one motion — do not hold the rebound for even half a second."
  - "PG: two steps after the catch, then dribble. Do not dribble before you catch."
  - "Wings: run to the corners, not to the elbow. If you stop at the elbow, you are in the lane, not in space."
- **Progressions:**
  - Easier: Walk through at half speed. Each player points to their next position before moving to it.
  - Harder: Add defenders who start at the far baseline and must sprint back. Offence must execute the outlet-and-fill sequence before the defenders recover.
  - Hardest: After the fill, PG reads the defence and either pushes for a primary break finish or calls the half-court set — full 5-on-5 transition.
