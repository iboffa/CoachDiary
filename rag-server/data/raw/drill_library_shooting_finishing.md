# Drill Library — Shooting and Finishing

## 1. Shooting and Finishing Drill Library Overview

Section summary: This section contains twelve drills covering catch-and-shoot, off-dribble shooting, post shooting, layup finishing, and floater finishing, each formatted with name, objective, setup, execution, coaching cues, and progressions from easier to harder.

---

## 2. Catch-and-Shoot Drills

### Drill SH-01: FIVE-SPOT CATCH-AND-SHOOT

- **Name:** Five-Spot Catch-and-Shoot
- **Objective:** Build the habit of setting the feet quickly on the catch and releasing a mechanically correct shot immediately without an extra dribble — the fundamental catch-and-shoot movement that underpins all spot-up scoring.
- **Setup:** Five cones (or tape marks) at the five classic shooting spots: north corner (approx. `{x: 87, y: 73}`), north wing (approx. `{x: 330, y: 113}`), top of the key (approx. `{x: 370, y: 255}`), south wing (approx. `{x: 330, y: 398}`), south corner (approx. `{x: 87, y: 438}`). One passer at the free-throw line with two balls. One shooter works through the spots.
- **Execution:**
  - Shooter starts at the north corner. Passer delivers a crisp pass timed to arrive as the shooter reaches the spot.
  - Shooter catches, sets feet, and shoots in one motion — no dribble allowed.
  - Shooter moves to the next spot immediately after shooting (regardless of make or miss).
  - Passer retrieves the ball and feeds the next spot. Use two balls so the pass is always ready.
  - One full rotation (all five spots) = one set. Complete five sets. Track makes: target 15 of 25 for developing players, 20 of 25 for competitive players.
- **Coaching cues:**
  - "Feet first, hands second" — the feet must be setting before the ball arrives.
  - "One fluid motion — catch to release without freezing at the top."
  - "Eyes to the target before the catch" — look at the basket before looking at the ball.
- **Progressions:**
  - Easier: Passer delivers the ball on the spot (stationary catch). Focus on the set feet and release mechanics only.
  - Harder: Shooter cuts from the paint to each spot before catching — the catch-and-shoot happens off movement, not from a stationary position.
  - Hardest: Closeout added — a third player closes out from the paint as the shooter catches. Shooter must make a shot-fake-and-one-dribble pull-up decision.

### Drill SH-02: FIVE-SPOT RAPID FIRE (TWO BALLS)

- **Name:** Five-Spot Rapid Fire
- **Objective:** Train volume shooting at game speed with a focus on quick releases and consistent mechanics under fatigue — the high-repetition volume develops muscle memory for the shooter's release.
- **Setup:** Same five spots as SH-01. Two passers: one at the free-throw line and one rebounding at the basket. Three balls in circulation.
- **Execution:**
  - Shooter fires shots continuously at each spot. As soon as a ball is released, the passer feeds the next ball.
  - Shooter takes three shots per spot then rotates clockwise.
  - No pause between shots at each spot — next ball arrives within two seconds of the previous release.
  - Track makes. One full circuit (five spots × three shots = 15 attempts) = one round. Complete four rounds per shooter.
- **Coaching cues:**
  - "Do not drop your arms between shots — your hands come back to shooting position as the pass arrives."
  - "Snap and follow through on every shot, even when fatigued."
  - "Call your shot — say 'yes' on makes, 'back' on misses, to build a mental reset habit."
- **Progressions:**
  - Easier: Two shots per spot, slower pass delivery. Focus on mechanics over speed.
  - Harder: Move spots to the three-point line. Maintain the same rapid-fire pace.
  - Hardest: Add a contested closeout on every third shot at each spot. Shooter must decide in real time: shoot over the closeout or use a jab-step and one-dribble pull-up.

### Drill SH-03: HORNS CATCH-AND-SHOOT (GAME CONTEXT)

- **Name:** Horns Catch-and-Shoot
- **Objective:** Practice catch-and-shoot mechanics within a set-play context — the shooter must read a screen, cut to the correct spot, and catch-and-shoot in one fluid motion, replicating the most common catch-and-shoot scenario in organised play.
- **Setup:** HORNS alignment (PG at top, PF and C at elbows, SG and SF in corners). Two balls. One coach or passer at the top of the key.
- **Execution:**
  - PG (1) simulates a ball screen action with C (5). C rolls. SG (2) reads: if "help comes from the corner side" (coach signals), SG cuts from the north corner to the north wing (approx. `{x: 330, y: 113}`) and catches a skip pass from the coach/PG for a three-point catch-and-shoot.
  - SF (3) is the alternate target on the opposite side.
  - Five makes per shooter before rotating.
- **Coaching cues:**
  - "Read the signal before you move — the cue triggers the cut, not the pass."
  - "Sprint to the spot, then set your feet — don't stop early and shuffle."
  - "Hands ready — your hands are in shooting position before the ball arrives."
- **Progressions:**
  - Easier: Remove the screen action; coach passes directly to SG at the wing. Focus on the catch-and-shoot only.
  - Harder: Add a live defender (X2) who must be beaten by the cut. SG must use the screen to get open.
  - Hardest: Add live defence on all five players and run the full HORNS play with competitive defence.

## 3. Off-Dribble Shooting Drills

### Drill SH-04: ONE-DRIBBLE PULL-UP (ELBOW SERIES)

- **Name:** One-Dribble Pull-Up Elbow Series
- **Objective:** Train the one-dribble pull-up jumper from the elbow area — the most common mid-range shot generated by ball screen, DHO, and isolation plays — emphasising balance on the pull-up landing and a consistent shooting pocket.
- **Setup:** One ball per player. Players line up at the top of the key. One cone at each elbow (approx. `{x: 190, y: 188}` and `{x: 190, y: 323}`).
- **Execution:**
  - Player dribbles from the top of the key toward the north cone (elbow).
  - At the cone, execute one decisive dribble left or right (alternating by round), then rise into a pull-up jumper from the elbow.
  - Focus: the single dribble creates lateral momentum; the player must gather that momentum into a balanced jump shot without taking a step off-balance.
  - Four shots at the north elbow, four at the south elbow. Three sets per player.
- **Coaching cues:**
  - "One dribble — not zero, not two. Commit to one."
  - "Gather on the second step after the dribble — the landing foot is balanced under your body."
  - "Eyes find the basket before the gather — don't look down at your feet."
- **Progressions:**
  - Easier: Stationary practice — stand at the elbow and shoot 10 mid-range jumpers without dribble approach. Focus only on mechanics.
  - Harder: Passer delivers the ball to the ball-handler at the top; ball-handler uses one dribble pull-up off the catch (simulates coming off a DHO).
  - Hardest: Add a defender at the cone who closes out. Ball-handler must shoot over or around the closeout on the pull-up.

### Drill SH-05: SPIN MOVE PULL-UP

- **Name:** Spin Move Pull-Up
- **Objective:** Train the spin (reverse pivot) dribble as an off-dribble shot creator — the player drives in one direction, uses a spin to create separation from the on-ball defender, and pulls up for a mid-range jumper before the help arrives.
- **Setup:** Cone at the north elbow (approx. `{x: 190, y: 188}`). One ball per player. Players line up at the top of the key.
- **Execution:**
  - Player drives hard from the top of the key to the north elbow cone.
  - At the cone: execute a reverse pivot (spin move) — plant the outside foot, rotate 180 degrees, emerge facing toward the three-point line or the lane.
  - Immediately pull up for a jumper in the first open window after the spin (at the elbow or at the mid-range area between the elbow and the wing).
  - Four repetitions driving right; four repetitions driving left. Three sets.
- **Coaching cues:**
  - "The spin is tight — your elbow stays close to your body; a wide spin bleeds momentum."
  - "Protect the ball through the spin — ball stays close to your hip."
  - "Quick gather after the spin — the pull-up happens on the first step out of the spin."
- **Progressions:**
  - Easier: Practise the spin move without a shot. Focus on the footwork and ball protection only.
  - Harder: Add a passive defender at the cone who forces the spin — defender takes away the straight drive, the spin is the only option.
  - Hardest: Add a second help defender at the pull-up location. Player must decide: pull up over the defender or kick out.

## 4. Post Shooting Drills

### Drill SH-06: DROP-STEP SERIES (POST)

- **Name:** Drop-Step Series
- **Objective:** Develop the two fundamental post-scoring moves — the drop step to the baseline and the drop step to the middle — and the decision between them based on where the defender is positioned.
- **Setup:** Center (C / 5) or Power Forward (PF / 4) on the south block (approx. `{x: 145, y: 323}`). Passer at the south wing (approx. `{x: 330, y: 398}`). One ball.
- **Execution:**
  - Passer enters the ball to the post player on the block.
  - Post player catches in a strong post position (both feet straddling the block, elbows out).
  - Round 1 (baseline drop step): Post player drops the baseline foot (the foot nearest the baseline) one step toward the baseline, seals the defender on their back, and finishes with a power layup.
  - Round 2 (middle drop step): Post player drops the middle foot one step toward the free-throw line, seals the defender, and finishes with a hook shot or jump hook.
  - Coach designates which drop step to use before the catch. Six reps per side (south block × three, north block × three) per round.
- **Coaching cues:**
  - "Read where your foot is — if your baseline foot is free, drop baseline. If your middle foot is free, drop middle."
  - "Seal first, then drop" — the post player must establish contact with the defender before the drop step; otherwise there is nothing to seal.
  - "Two hands on the ball through the drop step — protect it until the gather."
- **Progressions:**
  - Easier: No defender. Post player practises the footwork and finish mechanics. 10 reps each direction per set.
  - Harder: Add a passive defender who applies token resistance but does not steal. Post player must feel the contact and react.
  - Hardest: Add a live defender at the post. Post player reads the defender's position and chooses the correct drop-step direction.

### Drill SH-07: JUMP HOOK FROM BOTH SIDES

- **Name:** Jump Hook From Both Sides
- **Objective:** Develop a reliable one-hand jump hook from each block, which is the most fundamental high-percentage post shot because it is nearly unblockable due to the ball being released above the defender's reach on the opposite side of the body.
- **Setup:** Post player starts at the south block (approx. `{x: 145, y: 323}`). Passer at the free-throw line (approx. `{x: 280, y: 255}`). One ball.
- **Execution:**
  - Passer delivers the ball to the post player on the block.
  - Post player plants the baseline foot, pivots on it, and rises into a one-hand jump hook aimed at the backboard or directly at the basket.
  - The hook is released with the shooting arm extended fully above the head, ball released at the top of the jump. The non-shooting arm protects the ball during the gather.
  - Six reps from the south block (right-hand hook); six reps from the north block (left-hand hook). Three sets.
- **Coaching cues:**
  - "Step through, not away" — the plant foot steps toward the basket, not sideways.
  - "Extend the arm fully — a bent-arm hook is a blocked hook."
  - "Use the backboard from close range" — shots within two metres of the basket are higher-percentage off the glass.
- **Progressions:**
  - Easier: Stand one metre from the basket and practise the hook release without dribble or post entry. Focus only on the arm extension and wrist release.
  - Harder: Post player receives the pass and must execute the hook while being pushed off their preferred position by a defender applying legal contact.
  - Hardest: Post player receives the ball, reads the defender, chooses between drop step, jump hook, or up-and-under. Live one-on-one post play.

## 5. Layup and Finishing Drills

### Drill FI-01: MIKAN DRILL

- **Name:** Mikan Drill
- **Objective:** Build the fundamental two-hand or one-hand power layup from both sides of the basket repeatedly at close range — the highest-repetition close-range finishing drill in basketball, used daily at all levels.
- **Setup:** One player, one ball, directly under the basket (approx. `{x: 54, y: 255}`). No cones needed.
- **Execution:**
  - Player starts under the basket on the north side (right side of the hoop). Using left foot as the gather foot, player executes a right-hand layup off the backboard.
  - Without stopping, player catches their own ball (or retrieves the rebound) and steps to the south side (left side). Using right foot as gather foot, execute a left-hand layup.
  - Alternate sides continuously: right-hand layup, left-hand layup, right-hand, left-hand.
  - Target: 10 consecutive makes without a miss. Time target: 20 makes in 30 seconds.
- **Coaching cues:**
  - "Step, gather, rise" — the gather foot plants one step out from the basket; jumping from directly below the basket is a travel.
  - "Use the backboard" — aim at the square on the backboard, not the rim.
  - "Soft hands on the catch — don't fight the rebound, guide it."
- **Progressions:**
  - Easier: Perform without timing pressure. Focus on the correct footwork (left foot gathers for right-hand layup; right foot gathers for left-hand layup).
  - Harder: Reverse Mikan — player steps under the basket and finishes a reverse layup on the far side of the rim, using the rim as a shield.
  - Hardest: Power Mikan — player jumps and tip-taps the ball off the backboard repeatedly without catching it (both hands tip, alternating sides), building explosive power in the finish.

### Drill FI-02: EURO-STEP LAYUP SERIES

- **Name:** Euro-Step Layup Series
- **Objective:** Train the Euro-step footwork (two-step lateral avoid move) as a finishing skill against shot-blockers and help defenders in the paint — the Euro-step allows the ball-handler to navigate around a committed help defender.
- **Setup:** One cone at the paint edge (approx. `{x: 160, y: 255}`) representing a shot-blocker. Players line up at the north wing (approx. `{x: 330, y: 113}`).
- **Execution:**
  - Player drives from the north wing toward the cone at full speed.
  - First step: plant toward the cone (toward the south side of the basket).
  - Second step: cross to the north side of the basket (past the cone), finishing with a north-side layup.
  - The ball is cradled low between the two steps — the ball transfer must happen between the first and second step without an extra dribble.
  - Five reps from the north wing (step south, finish north). Five reps from the south wing (step north, finish south). Three sets.
- **Coaching cues:**
  - "Big first step, bigger second step" — the first step commits the defender; the second step is the avoid.
  - "Ball below the waist between steps" — a high ball between steps gets stripped by the shot-blocker.
  - "Finish with the near hand" — finish with the hand on the same side as the finish side.
- **Progressions:**
  - Easier: Walk through the footwork without a ball. First step → second step → jump → arm extension. Master the pattern before adding speed.
  - Harder: Replace the cone with a passive defender who jumps to block straight-line drives. Player must read and use the Euro-step at game speed.
  - Hardest: Add a second defender at the basket. Player must Euro-step past the first and finish through or around the second.

### Drill FI-03: FLOATER SERIES

- **Name:** Floater Series
- **Objective:** Develop a reliable off-hand and strong-hand floater (teardrop) shot from the mid-paint area — the floater allows shorter guards to finish over shot-blocking bigs who have committed to the rim.
- **Setup:** One ball per player. Players start at the top of the key (approx. `{x: 370, y: 255}`). One cone at the free-throw line (approx. `{x: 280, y: 255}`) representing a tall rim-protector.
- **Execution:**
  - Player drives from the top of the key toward the cone.
  - At the cone, gather into a one-step or two-step jump and release a high-arcing floater over the cone, targeting the rim (approx. `{x: 54, y: 255}`).
  - The floater is released at the peak of the jump with the wrist extended upward — the arc must be high enough to clear a hand at 2.5 metres.
  - Round 1: Strong-hand floater (right-hand drive, right-hand release). Round 2: Weak-hand floater (left-hand drive, left-hand release). Six reps per round, three rounds.
- **Coaching cues:**
  - "High arc, soft touch — the floater is not a layup; it is a miniature jump shot from the mid-paint."
  - "Release at the peak of the jump — not on the way up, not on the way down."
  - "Use your off hand to protect the ball on the gather — the shot-blocker is trying to strip the gather."
- **Progressions:**
  - Easier: Practise the floater release from a stationary position at the free-throw line. Focus on the high arc and wrist extension only.
  - Harder: Drive at full speed and add a defender who stands at the cone and raises one hand. Player must arc the floater over the raised hand.
  - Hardest: Add a trailing defender who applies pressure on the drive. Player must protect the ball on the gather and still release a clean floater.

### Drill FI-04: CONTACT LAYUP DRILL

- **Name:** Contact Layup Drill
- **Objective:** Train the ability to finish at the rim under legal contact — the most common source of missed layups in game conditions is unexpected contact that disrupts balance; this drill builds the proprioceptive stability to finish through contact.
- **Setup:** Two players, one ball. Ball-handler drives from the top of the key. Defender positions at the restricted area (approx. `{x: 90, y: 255}`) and applies a legal body bump on the drive (no reaching, no flagrant contact — straight-body vertical contact only).
- **Execution:**
  - Ball-handler drives at game speed from the top.
  - Defender takes a legal charge position and applies a bump as the ball-handler enters the restricted area.
  - Ball-handler must absorb the contact and finish the layup without losing the ball or stepping on the restricted area line.
  - Five reps per player in the offensive role. Switch roles.
- **Coaching cues:**
  - "Absorb with the core — tighten your midsection before contact, not after."
  - "Keep the ball above the defender's reach during the gather — a low ball on contact becomes a strip."
  - "Finish with the second step after contact — if the first step takes the bump, the second step is still free."
- **Progressions:**
  - Easier: Defender applies only light contact (a touch, not a bump). Ball-handler focuses on maintaining balance and the gather footwork.
  - Harder: Two defenders — one on the drive, one at the basket. Ball-handler must absorb the first contact and finish past the second.
  - Hardest: Full 1-on-1 from the three-point line — drive, contact, and finish at full game speed with a live defender making legal attempts to stop the finish.
